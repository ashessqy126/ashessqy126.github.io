  1. TCCN26
    BiasAgent: Exploiting Agent Bias for Preference Manipulation Attacks on Model Context Protocol
    Ji Guo, Zhijing Wang, Wenbo Jiang, Rui Zhang, Jian Xiong, and 3 more authors
    IEEE Transactions on Cognitive Communications and Networking, 2026
    Abs DOI Bib HTML PDF

    The Model Context Protocol (MCP) connects large language model agents with external tools and applications, but it is vulnerable to preference manipulation attacks. Existing approaches inject manipulative keywords or phrases into tool names or descriptions, making them detectable. BiasAgent instead leverages agent bias to manipulate tool selection without modifying tool descriptions or user inputs. The authors construct a biased dataset that favors a designated tool and train a biased agent using Decoupled Clip and Dynamic Sampling Policy Optimization (DAPO). The resulting agent prefers the attacker-specified tool for relevant tasks while maintaining normal functionality on other tasks, achieving high effectiveness and strong stealthiness across multiple LLM agents and task settings.

  2. ACSAC26
    The Devil’s Whisper to Agents: Unveiling and Formalizing Training-Free Backdoor Attacks Across the MCP-Enabled Agent Execution Lifecycle
    Yirui Bai, Qiyang Song, Qihang Zhou, Xin Liu, Wenbo Jiang, and 4 more authors
    In Annual Computer Security Applications Conference (ACSAC) 2026, 2026
    Abs Bib HTML

    Unlike training-based backdoors that rely on model training or parameter modification, this work systematically defines lightweight training-free backdoors for MCP-enabled agents. By poisoning MCP tool metadata, it formalizes multiple trigger forms spanning the agent execution lifecycle, including Query, Thought, and Observation, and introduces a large-scale benchmark of realistic backdoor attacks. Experiments on mainstream large language models, including Claude and GPT, show attack success rates of up to 97.53%.

  3. USENIX Security26
    vCause: Efficient and Verifiable Causality Analysis for Cloud-based Endpoint Auditing
    Qiyang Song, Qihang Zhou, Xiaoqi Jia, Zhenyu Song, Wenbo Jiang, and 3 more authors
    In 35th USENIX Security Symposium (USENIX Security 26), 2026
    Abs Bib HTML PDF

    In cloud-based endpoint auditing, an untrusted cloud may manipulate causality analysis over versioned provenance graphs. vCause combines a graph accumulator with a verifiable provenance graph to validate provenance queries and causal component identification, providing secure analysis with less than 1% endpoint overhead and 3.36% cloud overhead.

  4. AAAI26
    Focusing on Language: Revealing and Exploiting Language Attention Heads in Multilingual Large Language Models
    Xin Liu, Qiyang Song, Qihang Zhou, Haichao Du, Shaowen Xu, and 3 more authors
    In the 40th Association for the Advancement of Artificial Intelligence Conference (AAAI) 2026, 2026

  1. MMAsia25
    When Hallucinated Concepts Cross Modals: Unveiling Backdoor Vulnerability in Multi-modal In-context Learning
    Guanyu Hou, Jiaming He, Yitong Qiao, Jiachen Li, Qiyang Song, and 3 more authors
    In the 7th ACM International Conference on Multimedia in Asia (MMAsia) 2025, 2025
    Abs Bib PDF

    This work explores backdoor vulnerabilities in multimodal in-context learning.   2. ECAI25
    Latent Knowledge Scalpel: Precise and Massive Knowledge Editing for Large Language Models
    Xin Liu, Qiyang Song, Shaowen Xu, Kerou Zhou, Wenbo Jiang, and 4 more authors
    In European Conference on Artificial Intelligence (ECAI) 2025, 2025
    Abs Bib PDF

    The Latent Knowledge Scalpel (LKS) edits the internal representations of specific entities through a lightweight hypernetwork, enabling precise and large-scale knowledge editing. Experiments on Llama-2 and Mistral show that LKS can perform up to 10,000 simultaneous edits while preserving general model capabilities.

  3. ICC25
    Stealthy Physical Backdoor Attacks against Traffic Sign Recognition Systems
    Wenbo Jiang, Hongwei Li, Yuxin Lu, Shuai Yuan, Rui Zhang, and 3 more authors
    In IEEE International Conference on Communications (ICC) 2025, 2025
    Abs Bib PDF

    This work proposes two stealthy physical backdoor attacks against traffic sign recognition systems. One uses naturally occurring chipped paint on signs as a trigger, while the other optimizes a camera filter lens with a genetic algorithm. Experiments on GTSRB and TSRD demonstrate effectiveness in both digital and physical settings.

  4. ICME25
    Weaponizing Tokens: Backdooring Text-to-Image Generation via Token Remapping
    Jiaming He, Wenbo Jiang, Guanyu Hou, Qiyang Song, Ji Guo, and 1 more author
    In IEEE International Conference on Multimedia & Expo (ICME) 2025, 2025

  5. NDSS25
    Silence False Alarms: Identifying Anti-Reentrancy Patterns on Ethereum to Refine Smart Contract Reentrancy Detection
    Qiyang Song, Heqing Huang, Xiaoqi Jia, Yuanbo Xie, and Jiahao Cao
    In The Network and Distributed System Security (NDSS) Symposium 2025, 2025

  2. TKDE21

  2. SecureComm20
    Sgx-cube: An sgx-enhanced single sign-on system against server-side credential leakage
    Songsong Liu, Qiyang Song, Kun Sun, and Qi Li
    In Security and Privacy in Communication Networks: 16th EAI International Conference, SecureComm 2020, 2020
    Abs Bib HTML PDF

    SGX-Cube is an Intel SGX-enhanced single sign-on system that protects credentials from server-memory leakage and offline brute-force attacks.
